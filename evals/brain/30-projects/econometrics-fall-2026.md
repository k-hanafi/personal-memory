# Econometrics (fall 2026)

## Overview

Fall 2026 econometrics for undergrads at the university. The course
covers identification, simple and multiple regression, and a first
pass at panel methods. This is the filed syllabus note.

The assigned text is Markets in Numbers by Rhea Calder. See
[[switch-econometrics-textbook]] for why the book changed.

Office hours are on the course page. Lecture is twice a week.
Recitation is once a week. The course page also holds the slides.

## Weekly schedule

Week 1: review of expectation and variance.
Week 2: causality versus correlation, and research designs.
Week 3: simple regression and the residual.
Week 4: multiple regression and omitted variables.
Week 5: dummy variables and interactions.
Week 6: functional form and transformations.
Week 7: midterm in class, covering weeks 1 to 6.
Week 8: heteroskedasticity and robust standard errors.
Week 9: instrumental variables, first stage and exclusion.
Week 10: difference in differences.
Week 11: panel data and fixed effects.
Week 12: limited dependent variables, a first look.
Week 13: replication workshop and paper presentations.
Week 14: review session and leftover questions.

## Readings

Calder, Markets in Numbers, chapters 1 to 12 in order.
Skip the optional appendices unless a problem set names them.
One short applied paper is posted each week on the course page.
Students read the paper before the Thursday meeting.
A short note on each paper is due the night before class.

## Assignments

Eight problem sets, due Friday, lowest score dropped.
One replication memo, due in week 13.
A midterm in week 7, in class, closed notes.
A sit-down final in the exam period.
Problem sets go out on Monday. Late work follows the policy below.

## Policies

Late problem sets lose ten percent per day, weekends included.
Collaboration on method is fine. Copied writeups are not.
The midterm is 25 percent of the course grade.
The problem sets are 25 percent of the course grade.
The replication memo is 10 percent of the course grade.
The final exam weight is 40 percent.

## Software notes

The lab machines have the department stats package installed.
Alex posts the scripts used in lecture the same afternoon.
Students may use any package that can export a clean table.
No model writes a results section. Students write their own.
Bring a laptop on lab days. The lab has extras if one fails.

## TA notes

The TA runs a weekly recitation and holds two office hours.
Questions on software go to the TA first.
Alex handles grading disputes and the replication memo.
Recitation is not optional in the first four weeks.
